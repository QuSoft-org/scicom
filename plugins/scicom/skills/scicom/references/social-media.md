# Social media

## Landscape (as of 2026; check again each year)

- Social and video platforms are now the most common way people reach online news: 54%, against 51% for news websites and apps (average of 48 markets, Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2026).
- 77% watch online news video every week. 10% use AI chatbots for news, 16% of under-35s.
- **Netherlands:** TV news is still level with online video. The top platforms for news are Facebook 27%, WhatsApp 24%, Instagram 20%, YouTube 20%, TikTok 9% and LinkedIn 7% (DNR 2026).
- Many academics moved from X to Bluesky between 2023 and 2025. Journalists and policymakers are spread across several platforms, so check where your specific audience actually is.
- Platforms change fast. Treat these numbers as a starting point.

## Rules for every post

- **Hook in the first line:** the finding, a surprising fact, a question with stakes, or a person. On most platforms only one or two lines show before "more".
- **Put the finding in the post itself.** Never rely on the click.
- **One idea per post.**
- **A visual with alt text.** Real people and real labs beat stock images.
- **People and process:** who did it, how, and what went wrong along the way.
- **Plain language** in short paragraphs, with one to three specific hashtags.
- **Tag** co-authors, partner institutions, funders and the journal, where they are on the platform.
- **Link last,** to the news item or the paper.
- **No "Happy to share…".** Start with what the reader gets.
- **Respond:** reply to questions, thank people, and correct errors openly.
- **Researchers' own accounts** often outperform institutional ones. Support researchers with templates and images, and let institutional accounts amplify them.
- **Institutional role:** researchers posting as staff follow the institution's and funder's guidelines. On politically sensitive topics, involve the communications office.
- **Breaking news:** check scheduled posts, and make sure someone can pause them.

## Platform notes

| Platform | Main audiences | What works | Length and format |
|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | industry, funders, policy, alumni, prospective staff and PhD candidates | first-person posts by researchers; lessons and implications; document or carousel posts | about 80–250 words; the first two lines decide; image or PDF carousel |
| Bluesky, Mastodon, X | researchers, science journalists, some policy people | threads with one point per post; figures; joining conversations | 300 characters on Bluesky, 500 by default on Mastodon, 280 on free X; 3–8 posts; the first post must stand alone |
| Instagram | students, prospective students, the public | behind the scenes; carousels with one point per slide; Reels | 5–10 slides; caption of about 150 words at most; alt text |
| TikTok, Reels, YouTube Shorts | younger audiences, the public | a face talking to camera; a hook within 1–2 seconds; one question answered; captions; real settings. About 60 seconds of lecture-style explaining can do well | 15–90 seconds, vertical, captions burned in |
| YouTube | search-driven viewers, teachers, students | explainers, lectures, lab tours; searchable titles, chapters, thumbnails | 3–15 minutes; transcript |
| Facebook, WhatsApp channels | local community, parents, older audiences | events, local relevance, community news | short and visual |

Who appears on camera matters: representation affects engagement and whether viewers can picture themselves as scientists. Show a range of researchers.

## Content beyond papers

- One figure explained.
- What we got wrong, and what we learned.
- Instruments and methods.
- A day in the lab.
- A common myth and the evidence.
- Q&A with a PhD candidate.
- Events, before and after.
- Vacancies with the human side.
- Milestones framed by what they enable.
- Explaining news in your field of expertise. Stay within it.

## Grant, award and milestone posts

- **Lead with what the money or role makes possible,** and why it matters. Then who, how much and from whom.
- **No "happy to share" or "proud to announce".**
- **Check the announcement date first:** has the funder announced the results publicly? Many funders set a date before which recipients may not post.
- **Name the funder and programme correctly,** tag them, and follow their visibility rules for logos.
- **End with a useful action,** such as a vacancy or a link to the news item.

## One paper, many posts

Start from the fact sheet and the main message (see `SKILL.md`), then spread out over several weeks, with a different angle each time (curiosity, people, method, implication, misconception):

1. News item on the website.
2. LinkedIn post in the researcher's voice.
3. Thread on Bluesky, Mastodon or X.
4. Instagram carousel: hook → problem → what we did → finding → why it matters.
5. 45–60-second video.
6. Newsletter paragraph.
7. Later: a "six months on" post, or the slide from a talk.

## Thread template

1. The finding, why it matters, and a visual. This post must work on its own.
2. The problem or context.
3. What the team did, with one concrete detail.
4. The key result: a figure with alt text.
5. Limits and next steps.
6. Credits (tags) and the link.

## EU funding and visibility

For EU-funded work, follow the grant agreement's visibility rules: the EU emblem and funding statement on communication materials, including social media visuals where feasible. Tag the programme's or funder's accounts. Rules differ between programmes, so check the current agreement.

## Moderation and safety

- Visible researchers increasingly face hostile comments, harassment and threats, especially on contested topics. Women and early-career researchers are targeted more often (Mede 2026).
- **Before posting on a sensitive topic:** agree who monitors and responds, prepare a short factual reply, and decide when to ignore, mute, block or report.
- Publish house rules for comments, use keyword filters, and answer genuine questions.
- Don't argue with trolls. When correcting misinformation, write for the people reading along, not for the attacker (see `uncertainty-risk-misinfo.md`).
- Keep private details (home address, private phone) off public profiles.
- Report threats to the institution's security or HR team. In the Netherlands, also use WetenschapVeilig.

## Measure what matters

Match the metric to the objective:
- impressions: awareness;
- comments and shares: engagement;
- clicks: interest;
- shares by journalists, policymakers and peers, and pickups in news or policy documents: influence;
- applications or registrations: behaviour;
- quality of conversation: listening.

Compare formats and topics over time rather than judging single posts.
