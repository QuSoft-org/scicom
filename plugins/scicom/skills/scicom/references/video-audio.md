# Video and audio

## Explainer video script

- **Length:**
  - 60–90 seconds for social media and the web, about 150–220 spoken words at roughly 140–150 words a minute;
  - 3–20 minutes for YouTube explainers, where viewers come on purpose.
- **Structure:**
  1. Hook (first 0–5 seconds): a question, a surprising image, or what is at stake.
  2. The problem: why it matters.
  3. What the team did or found, with one concrete visual.
  4. Why it matters and what happens next.
  5. An ask (follow, read, apply) or a closing image.
- **Write for the ear:**
  - short sentences, one idea each;
  - simple numbers;
  - repeat the key point;
  - read it aloud and time it.
- **Two-column script:** what we see on the left (shots, graphics, on-screen text), what we hear on the right (voice, sound, music). Template in `templates.md`.
- **Researchers on camera or in podcasts:** give them talking points and questions, not a verbatim script. Over-scripted delivery seems less authentic. Keep the full script for voice-over.
- **Show, don't tell:** lab footage, hands at work, before and after, simple animation for what cannot be seen.
- **Always add captions.** Much social video is watched without sound. Keep on-screen text short.
- **End card:** names, affiliations, funder, link.

## Filming basics (Reel Life Science; Frontiers)

- **Sound first.** Viewers forgive a poor image sooner than poor sound. Use a clip-on microphone, a quiet room, and keep the microphone close to the speaker. Outdoors, use a windshield or record the voice-over indoors.
- **Light:** face the light, such as a window or lamp in front. Avoid strong light from behind.
- **Framing:**
  - eyes in the upper third, camera at eye level, steady on a tripod; move closer rather than zooming;
  - a tidy background with a hint of the lab or office.
- **Orientation:** vertical (9:16) for TikTok, Reels and Shorts; horizontal (16:9) for YouTube and websites.
- **Takes:** several short takes beat one long one. Film extra footage too: equipment, people working, close-ups.
- **Permissions:**
  - consent from everyone who can be identified;
  - check lab safety rules;
  - check for confidential material in shot (whiteboards, screens);
  - licensed music only.

## Short vertical video (TikTok, Reels, Shorts)

- **Hook within one or two seconds:** say or show the surprising point.
- **A person talking to camera** in a real setting. Authentic beats polished.
- **Format:** one question answered per video; captions burned in. Talking explainers about 60–90 seconds, demonstrations 30 seconds or less: demonstrations for views, explainers for comments and questions (Zawacki et al. 2022; Habibi & Salim 2021).
- **What works:** tie videos to a current event (posted within a day) or a place viewers know. Use a human voice, on camera or as voice-over, rather than text over music.
- **Who appears:** see the representation line in `social-media.md`.

## Podcasts

- **Choose a format:** interview, conversation, narrative documentary, or short explainer.
- **Prepare:**
  - brief the guest, and agree two or three key points and stories;
  - prepare open questions: "What surprised you?", "What does a normal day look like?", "What went wrong?".
- **Record** in a quiet room with decent microphones. If remote, record each speaker separately.
- **Edit** for clarity but keep it natural.
- **Plan a limited season** on a fixed schedule: a third of science podcasts stop before ten episodes (MacKenzie 2019). Film the recording too; many podcasts now also run as video.
- **Publish** with show notes (summary, links, credits) and a transcript, for accessibility and search.
