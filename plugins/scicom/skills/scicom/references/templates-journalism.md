# Templates for journalists

Fill-in templates for the `journalist` role. Rules are in `journalism.md`.

## Story plan (before reporting)

```
What the story is really about: …
Who is affected, and what they need to know to decide for themselves: …
Who has the information; who can give context: …
Stakeholders beyond authors and press office (users, patients, technicians, regulators, competitors, funders): affected | invested
Form (news / explainer / profile / scene / data / audio) and what it needs (images, a telling detail, the dataset, tape): …
Expected lede before reporting (for the hypothesis test): …
```

## News story

```
Headline: the finding, its stage word (preprint / in mice / lab prototype), no hype
Lede: the news, declaratively (a short anecdote only when the news isn't urgent)
Nut graf: why this matters now, plus the main caveat
Evidence and method: what was done, on what, how many; source linked
Outside voice: independent expert, weighted by evidence
Limitations and open questions: what hasn't been shown or done yet (no independent test, data not shared); company claims attributed ("the company says…")
Funding and interests: who paid; authors' ties
What's next: only what the researchers state
```

## Interview questions

```
Authors
- What is the main point, and how does it advance the field?
- What earlier work does it build on, and how does it compare with recent competing work?
- What are the limitations? What is preliminary? What surprised you?
- What do you know for sure, and what is still open?
- Does the press release say more than your data support?
- What timeline would you give a colleague?
- What evidence would change your view? Which other explanations fit the same data?
- Who funded it, and did the funder have any say?
- Who else should I ask, including someone who will disagree?
- What analogy do you use with students? What haven't I asked that I should have?
- Character: have you seen a case like this before? How do you work on something like this?
(Order: background interviews first, the key source once you know the field, hardest questions last.)

Outside experts
- What is new compared with the latest similar work?
- Is the method sound? What is missing?
- Would you act on it yet? Who stands to gain?
- The strongest objection: [critic's point]. How do the authors answer it?

Contested topics (residents, farmers, activists)
- What gets oversimplified about this? Where do you feel torn?
- Does anything the other side says make sense to you? What do you think they think of you?
- What experiences shaped your view?
```

## Expert-finding note

```
Question the expert must address: …
Candidates (each with a verifiable link): name | institution | recent paper on this question | ties to the study or sponsor | checked: register / payments
Routes still to try: paper's references · recent papers · SMC · authors' suggestions · "who disagrees?"
```

## Study assessment

Fill in the red-flag list from `journalism.md` ("Assessing a study or press release"): paper versus release · design and comparator · sample · stage and venue · effect · authors · funding · company claims · fit with earlier work.

## Fact-check memo

```
Claim (as in draft) | Source (section/figure, timestamp, document) | Source's knowledge (seen / first-hand / second-hand / release) | How verified | Status (OK / changed / cut / ask)
```

## Filing note to the desk

```
Qualifiers that must survive the edit: …
Strongest headline the evidence supports: …   Options: 1. … 2. … 3. …
Stage word for the headline: …
Changes from the pitch: …
Hypothesis test: expected lede … / final lede … (flag if they barely differ)
Follow-up trigger (outcome, verdict, publication): …
Open checks before publication: …
AI-use record: …
```

## Reply request

```
Subject: Request for response: [topic], [outlet], deadline [date, time]
We are preparing a [news story / feature] on [topic] for publication on [date].
It will say that: 1. … 2. … (specific claims)
We would like your response by [deadline]. If we don't hear from you, we will say that comment was sought.
[Name, outlet, phone]
```

## Correction note

```
[Date] An earlier version of this article [said / omitted] [the error]. In fact, [the correct fact]. [What changed in the text.]
```

## Freelance pitch to an editor

```
Subject: Pitch: [working headline]
Timing, location, embargo: …
Opening: the lede you would write, under a working headline
Summary (about 100 words): the story, why now, why this outlet's readers need it
The questions the piece will answer: …
Sources: named interviewees, at least one independent; paper and release links
Format and length; page furniture (boxes, graphics)
About me: credentials, two or three clips
Follow-up: after about a day for news, about a week for features; one outlet at a time
```
