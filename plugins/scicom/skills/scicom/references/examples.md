# Worked examples (fictional)

All examples are **fictional**: invented studies, people and institutions, made up to show the method. Never present them, or parts of them, as real.

## 1. A physics paper becomes a press release opening and a thread

**Input (fictional):**
- Paper in a peer-reviewed journal.
- A lab prototype of a light detector made from a thin layer of a new material.
- It registers single particles of light at minus 270 °C, with 80% efficiency, against about 50% for an older design in the same tests.
- Tested in one lab. The authors say practical use needs testing at higher temperatures.

**Fact sheet decisions:**
- Evidence type: lab experiment, prototype.
- Limitation: very cold conditions, one lab.
- Stated next step: test at higher temperatures.

**Headline:**
- *Rejected:* "Breakthrough detector set to revolutionise quantum internet".
- *Chosen:* "New detector catches more single particles of light in lab tests".

**Lede:**
> A prototype light detector built at the [fictional] Institute of Applied Physics caught eight out of ten single particles of light in laboratory tests, compared with about five for an earlier design. Such sensitive detectors are needed for secure communication and precise measurements.

**Why it works:**
- The headline and lede carry the finding and the conditions ("in lab tests", "prototype").
- The comparison is given as a natural frequency ("eight out of ten").
- There is no application timeline.

**Thread, first post:**
> Single particles of light are easy to lose. Our new detector catches 8 in 10 of them in the lab, up from about 5 in 10 for the older design. Why that matters, and what it can't do yet 🧵 [image with alt text]

**Thread, limits post:**
> Big caveat: this works at −270 °C, in one lab. Next we test it at higher temperatures.

## 2. Fixing a hyped draft (a correlational study)

**Input (fictional):** an observational study finds that students who sleep more than eight hours score higher in maths tests.

**Draft headline:** "Sleep makes you better at maths, scientists prove".

**Review, the three changes that matter most:**
1. "Makes you" and "prove" claim causation, but the study is observational. Write "is linked to".
2. No study type or size given. Add "in a study of 1 200 secondary-school students in three countries" [CHECK].
3. The quote says "every school should start later". That advice goes beyond the evidence. Ask the researcher to reword it, or add context from an independent expert.

**Revised headline:** "More sleep linked to higher maths scores in secondary-school students".

**Revised lede:**
> Secondary-school students who slept more than eight hours a night scored higher on maths tests than those who slept less, a study of 1 200 students [CHECK] found. The study shows a link, not that sleep causes the higher scores: other factors, such as family routines, may play a role.

## 3. A grant announcement on LinkedIn

**Before:**
> Happy to share that I received a €1.5M grant from [funder]!! So grateful to my amazing team 🙏 #grant #research

**After, in the researcher's voice:**
> Every year, bridges across Europe need inspections that close roads for days [CHECK]. With a new €1.5 million grant from [funder] [CHECK], my team will spend five years [CHECK] developing sensors that let a bridge report its own cracks, without closing the road.
>
> Our first step: [CHECK: the researcher's own first step, e.g. testing the sensors on a decommissioned bridge].
>
> Thanks to [co-applicants, tagged] and to [funder, tagged].
>
> We are hiring two PhD candidates: [link]
>
> #civilengineering #sensors

**Why it works:**
- It leads with the problem and what the money makes possible.
- The milestone comes second, followed by a concrete next step.
- It credits people and funders, and ends with a useful action (the vacancy).

## 4. A policy brief that acts as honest broker

**Context (fictional):** the regional government of [fictional region] asks a university team how to cut the energy use of public buildings. The values involved are contested: costs, comfort and climate targets pull in different directions.

**Role choice:** honest broker. Present options, not one recommendation, because the trade-offs depend on political priorities.

**Key messages box:**
> - Three options could cut energy use by roughly 10–35% by 2035 [CHECK model ranges]; they differ in cost, speed and disruption.
> - **Option A, better controls:** low cost, a fast start, about 10–15% savings.
> - **Option B, insulation programme:** higher cost, 5–8 years, about 20–30% savings, and disruption during works.
> - **Option C, heat pumps combined with B:** highest cost, the largest savings (about 25–35%), and it depends on grid capacity.
> - The savings estimates come from models of 40 representative buildings, with an uncertainty of about ±5 percentage points.

**Why it works:**
- The options are stated with their trade-offs and uncertainty.
- It makes no hidden recommendation.
- The numbers are ranges, and they are marked for checking against the model.
