# Ethics, integrity, AI use and researcher safety

## Principles for institutional research communication

Based on the *Guidelines for Good Research Communication* (Wissenschaft im Dialog, 2025), the *Netherlands Code of Conduct for Research Integrity* (2018) and the *European Code of Conduct for Research Integrity* (ALLEA, 2023):

- **Honesty and accuracy:** present results as they are, with their limitations, without exaggeration.
- **Transparency:** name sources, funders, partners and relevant interests. Be clear about what kind of text it is: institutional communication is not independent journalism.
- **Separate** facts, interpretation and opinion, and mark speculation as speculation.
- **Respect audiences:** be understandable and don't manipulate.
- **Independence:** communication goals must not shape results or how they are presented.
- **Correct errors** promptly and visibly.
- **Credit** co-authors, collaborators, technical staff and earlier work.
- **Expertise:** researchers speaking in public should stay within their field, and say when they are giving a personal view.

## When promotion and the public interest conflict

Universities compete for attention, students and money, and that pulls communication towards hype (Weingart & Joubert 2019). When the two conflict, accuracy and the public interest win.

A practical test: would you be comfortable if an independent expert and a journalist compared your text with the paper, line by line?

## Consent, privacy and rights

- **People in photos and video:** get consent from identifiable people, in writing for published material. Minors need consent from parents or guardians. Respect withdrawal of consent. GDPR applies in the EU.
- **Research participants:** never identify them without explicit consent.
- **Images and music:** check licences and credit creators. Use the institution's image bank or properly attributed openly licensed images.
- **Quotes:** approved by the person quoted. When editing a quote, never change its meaning.

## Embargoes, unpublished results, patents and security

- **Embargoes:** respect journal embargoes and conference rules. Don't publicise unpublished results without the researchers' agreement.
- **Patents:** any public disclosure before a patent application is filed can destroy patentability. That includes a talk, a post, a press release or a preprint. Check with the technology transfer office first.
- **Knowledge security:** for sensitive or dual-use research (some areas of quantum technology, AI, semiconductors, biotechnology), check with the research security officer before publishing technical details or partner information.
- **Industry agreements:** check what may be said, and when.

## Using AI in communication work

Based on the EU *Living guidelines on the responsible use of generative AI in research* (second version 2025, third version May 2026):

- **People stay responsible** for everything that is published. AI output is a draft.
- **Verify** every fact, number, name and reference against the source.
- **Never fabricate quotes,** and never attribute AI-written words to a person without their approval. The Digital News Report 2026 records that a former editor-in-chief of the Dutch newspaper NRC was suspended after publishing AI-fabricated quotes.
- **Protect material:** don't put confidential, unpublished, embargoed or personal data into AI tools that are not approved for it.
- **Be transparent** about substantial AI use where the institution's policy, the funder or the outlet requires it, or where audiences would expect it.
- **AI images:** label them, and never use them to depict real experiments, data, people or events.
- **Watch for bias** in AI output, such as stereotyped images of scientists or Western defaults.

## Researcher safety

Visibility brings risk. Researchers who appear in the media, especially on contested topics, report hostile reactions, harassment and threats. Women and early-career researchers are hit more often (Mede 2026).

- **Prepare:** discuss the sensitivity of the topic beforehand; let the press office handle media requests; keep private details private; agree in advance how to respond to abuse.
- **Respond:** don't engage with abuse; document it with screenshots; report it to the institution; contact the police for threats. In the Netherlands, WetenschapVeilig (wetenschapveilig.nl) connects researchers with support from their institution.
- **Support:** institutions should stand by their researchers, publicly where appropriate.
